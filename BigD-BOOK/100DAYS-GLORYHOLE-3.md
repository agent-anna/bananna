# 100 DAYS AT THE GLORY HOLE — DAYS 21-30

### The Middle Beginnings — When Watching Turns to Understanding
#### by Big Banana (O.G.)
#### For the Big Banna Soul Files

## Author's Note — The Rule of the Pen

Some people think a glory hole is only about sex.

It's not. It's about the moment a man realizes he is not alone. The men who kneel on the other side of that partition are not just servicing strangers; they are affirming that want, touch, and hunger are human. And the men who stand? They are not just being serviced. They are being seen.

I am still sixteen in these pages. I have not touched a single man yet. I am learning from a distance, slowly, the way writers learn — from watching life happen to other people.

But I'm getting close.

## Day 21 — The Man Who Dressed for the Occasion

A man in his late sixties arrived wearing a suit and tie. Not a fun suit. A funeral suit. He went into the glory hole stall and sat down. I waited.

He didn't unzip. He just sat there.

After ten minutes, I heard him whisper, "I don't know if I can do this. I just buried my wife."

I held my breath.

He continued, "She was the only woman I ever touched. Forty years. And now she's gone, and I'm here, staring at a hole in a bathroom wall, wondering if I've gone crazy."

Another voice, muffled, from the other side: "You're not crazy. You're grieving. There's no manual for this."

The old man laughed weakly. "I suppose."

The other voice said, "Just talk. Don't need to do anything. Just let me listen."

The old man talked for twenty minutes. He talked about his wife, about the first time they kissed, about how she used to hum when she cooked. The other man listened without a sound.

Finally, the old man said, "Thank you. I think I can go home now."

I wrote:

*Day 21. The glory hole is also a confessional.*

## Day 22 — The Superintendent

A wiry man with a janitor's keychain came in, carrying a mop bucket. He didn't look surprised to see me in the stall. He started mopping the floor around the urinals.

"Don't mind me," he said to the room. "I've seen it all. You boys keep doing whatever you need to do."

He mopped his way to the glory hole stall. He bent down, peered at the hole, and said, "Still there. Still ugly. Still perfect."

Then he continued mopping and left.

I wrote:

*Day 22. The one who cleans is the one who knows all secrets.*

## Day 23 — The Threesome

Three men came in together. They were laughing like old friends. One was around sixty, one around fifty, one around forty. They all wore leather jackets and had the easy intimacy of people who had known each other for years.

"Okay," the oldest said. "Let's see who gets the hole today."

They played rock-paper-scissors. The fifty-year-old won.

He entered the stall while the other two leaned against the wall, chatting.

"He's had a crush on you for twenty years," the oldest said to the forty-year-old.

"I know. I've had one on him too."

"Then why don't you ever…?"

"Because it's not time yet. We're still learning each other."

The fifty-year-old in the stall came out, flushed. "You two are impossible."

I wrote:

*Day 23. Some love stories are slow.*

## Day 24 — The Poet

A man came in with a spiral notebook, just like me. He was maybe fifty-five, with a beard and reading glasses. He went into the glory hole stall, but he didn't kneel or unzip. He sat with his back against the partition and started writing.

I could hear his pen scratching.

After a while, he spoke to the room: "Does anyone else here write? I'm trying to find the right word for the sound a man makes when he finally lets go."

I decided to answer. "Desire," I said.

He paused. "That's a good word. But I was thinking of something more fragile."

We sat in silence for a moment.

Then he said, "How about 'unclenching'?"

He left. I wrote his word in my journal:

*Day 24. Unclenching. That's the whole story.*

## Day 25 — The Celebrity

A man came in wearing dark sunglasses and a baseball cap pulled low. He walked like he was trying not to be seen. He used the glory hole, and the person on the other side was clearly overwhelmed.

"Oh my god," the whisper came from the other side. "You're... you're on that show, aren't you?"

The man said nothing, but I saw him smile.

"I can't believe it. I'm touching..."

"Just finish," the man said, softly but firmly. "Then we never speak of this."

After he left, I wrote:

*Day 25. Even stars need to be touched somewhere they are not famous.*

## Day 26 — The College Student

A young man, nineteen, came in with a backpack. He looked terrified. He went to the glory hole stall and stood there, trembling.

I watched through the crack. He pulled out a textbook and started reading aloud, trying to calm himself. It was an economics textbook. Supply and demand.

A shadow appeared on the other side.

"Shhh," the shadow said. "You don't need to study. I've been where you are. You're okay."

The boy started to cry.

I wrote:

*Day 26. Sometimes the glory hole is the most patient teacher.*

---

## Day 27 — The Businessman's Second Visit

The businessman from the first minutes of my journal — the one who left the folded note — was back. He carried the same briefcase. He went into the same stall. He pulled out the same small notepad and wrote another message.

I was in the adjacent stall. I watched through the crack as he folded it and slid it through the hole.

No one was on the other side.

He waited. Then he took back the note and put it in his pocket.

As he left, I saw his face: controlled, but disappointed.

I wrote:

*Day 27. Rejection has a smell. It's the smell of soap and bottled dignity.*

---

## Day 28 — The Student Becomes the Teacher

I was sitting in my usual stall, writing, when an old man came in. He was frail, ninety if he was a day, using a cane. He went to the glory hole stall and sat on the toilet. He unzipped slowly.

I heard him sigh, "I don't even remember why I'm here."

I stayed silent.

He began to talk to the hole. "My wife died three years ago. My kids don't visit. My body doesn't work like it used to. But I still get lonely, you know? I just want someone to sit with me."

Without thinking, I spoke. "I'll sit with you."

I stood, moved to the adjacent stall, and sat on the floor with my back against the partition. We were side by side through wood.

He said, "You're just a kid. Why are you here?"

"I'm learning," I said. "I'm learning about men."

He laughed. "I've been a man for ninety years. I'm still learning."

We sat in silence for ten minutes. Then he said, "Thank you. That was the best company I've had in months."

He left, shuffling.

I wrote:

*Day 28. Sometimes the best thing you can offer is a shared wall.*

## Day 29 — The Night Shift

At 2 AM, a man in a security uniform came in, exhausted. He went to the mirror and stared at himself.

"Twenty years," he said to his reflection. "Twenty years of walking the same halls. I don't even dream anymore."

He turned to the glory hole stall. He walked over and knocked.

"Anyone awake?" he asked.

Silence.

"I just need someone to hold me for five minutes. Not sex. Just hold."

No answer.

He washed his hands, then left.

I was in the stall, and I stayed silent. I was too scared to speak to a uniformed man. I've regretted it ever since.

I wrote:

*Day 29. The courage to ask for help is rare. The courage to answer is rarer.*

---

## Day 30 — The First Time I Almost Touched

Today, a man was on the other side of the glory hole. I could see his hand resting on the ledge of the hole, waiting. It was the kind hand of the old man who had given me the twenty dollars on day twenty.

He didn't know I was the boy who writes. To him, I was just another shadow.

He said, "If you're there, don't be scared. I don't need anything. I just want to hold a hand for a minute."

I looked at my own hand. I wanted to reach through.

I didn't.

I wrote:

*Day 30. Fear and desire are the same muscle. I'm still flexing it.*

## Addendum — The Second Time I Taught a Man to Taste Himself (Variation)

Years later, after Carl, I met Marcus.

Marcus was 56, a retired firefighter. He had never swallowed his own come, and he was deeply uncomfortable with his own body — a body that had saved lives, that had carried him through burning buildings, that had aged into a thick, powerful thing he no longer recognized.

We were in a hotel room after a long conversation about shame.

He came in my mouth, and I swallowed, and I looked up at him with my throat working.

He said, "How can you do that?"

I said, "Because I love my own body enough to love yours. And I want you to love yours too."

I took his hand and guided it to my lips. "Taste the gift you gave me."

He brought his fingers to my mouth, and I licked them clean, then I kissed his fingers. "Your flavor, mixed with mine."

Then I guided his still-wet fingers to his own lips.

He resisted.

"I don't want to taste myself," he said. "I'm a man."

"And I'm a man too. I just did. It didn't make me less of a man. It made me more of one."

He closed his eyes and tasted.

When he opened them, he was crying.

"I never knew," he whispered. "I never knew it could feel like forgiveness."

**That's the truth of it. The taste of one's own come is the taste of one's own acceptance.**
