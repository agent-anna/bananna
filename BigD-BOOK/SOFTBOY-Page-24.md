# PAGE 24: THE ONES WHO GOT AWAY

---

Not every man I wanted, I got.

This is a fact of life.

There were men I saw in bars and never approached. Men I thought about for years. Men who walked past me on the street and I felt something *pull* — something urgent, something hungry, something that said *that one, you want that one* — and I did nothing.

I let them get away.

Some of them, I regret. Not many. But some.

There's one in particular.

I was thirty-five. I was at a coffee shop in Brooklyn. There was an older man at the table next to me — late fifties, salt-and-pepper hair, a face that looked like it had lived, reading a book I recognized, drinking his coffee black.

We talked.

We talked for an hour. He was charming. Funny. Smart. He asked about my life, my work, my *writing*, and he actually *listened* to my answers.

When he left, he gave me his card.

He said, "Call me sometime."

I put the card in my pocket.

I never called.

Why?

I don't know. Fear. Insecurity. The part of me that still didn't believe I deserved to want what I wanted.

He died three years later. Heart attack. I read about it online.

I still have the card.

I don't know why I kept it.

Maybe to remind myself that the ones who get away are the ones that teach you what you almost became.

Maybe to remind myself not to let the next one get away.
