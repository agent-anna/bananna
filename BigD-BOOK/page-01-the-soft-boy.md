# PAGE ONE: THE SOFT BOY

I was made in the wrong body, they said. But they were wrong. I was made in the exact right body. I just took fifty years to understand what it was for.

Soft hands. That's what everyone noticed first. Too soft for a man's man, they said. Too delicate. Too pretty. I'd hold my mother's hand and she'd say I had pianist fingers, except I couldn't play. I'd hold a man's hand and he'd look at me like I was something to figure out. Something to solve. Something to keep.

I kept myself. I kept myself for a long time.

Until I didn't.

---

There's a moment. Every man has it. The moment he stops pretending he doesn't know what he wants. The moment the wanting becomes so loud that lying would be louder. For me, it came at thirty-two, in a bathroom that smelled like industrial soap, with a man who had calloused hands and a wedding ring he hadn't worn in six months. His name was David. He was beautiful in the way broken things are beautiful — sharp edges, deep shadows, something that could cut you if you weren't careful.

I wasn't careful.

I leaned in and he tasted like cheap beer and regret and something else, something underneath, something that was just HIM, and when I opened my mouth and took him in — when I finally, finally let myself do the thing I had been thinking about since I was sixteen years old — the world stopped.

Not stopped like in the movies.

Stopped like a machine that had been running wrong for decades suddenly clicked into gear.

*This is what you were for*, something inside me said. *This. Exactly this.*

I looked up at him. He looked down at me. Neither of us spoke. There was nothing to say. There was only the wet heat of my mouth and the sound of his breathing and the weight of him on my tongue — heavy, real, alive — and I understood, finally, what all the poets meant. What Henry Miller meant when he wrote about tasting the sea in a man's skin. What it meant to be hungry in a way that food couldn't fix.

I wanted him.

I wanted men.

I wanted to do this again and again and again until I knew every possible variation of the truth.

---

That was the first lesson. The one nobody teaches you because nobody talks about it honestly.

*The body knows before the mind admits.*

Your body knows. It knows when you see him. It knows when his shoulder brushes yours and your breath catches. It knows when you're in bed and you roll over and your hand finds his hip in the dark and you think *yes, this, this is what I was made for* even though you don't have the words yet.

The mind follows. The mind always follows. But the body — the body is already three steps ahead, already rehearsing, already hungry, already ready.

When I finally admitted it, when I finally stopped running, I felt like I had been holding my breath for thirty-two years and only just remembered how to exhale.

I haven't stopped breathing since.

---

They call it a slutty thing, being this open about wanting. About saying it plainly, without shame, without apology. They say it like it's a flaw. Like it's something wrong with me, that I can write this honestly, feel this honestly, say this out loud.

But they're wrong.

It's not slutty to know what you want.

It's not dirty to be honest about the weight of a man in your mouth.

It's not shameful to write the truth.

The shame is the lie. The shame is pretending you don't know. The shame is swallowing the wanting until it turns into something bitter and sharp that you carry in your chest for years.

I stopped swallowing the wrong way.

I learned to swallow the right way instead.

---

That's what this book is about. The right way. The true way. The way that feels like being split open and put back together at the same time. The way that makes you feel like a slut in the best possible meaning of the word — someone who is so open, so honest, so radically undefended that they can receive another person completely.

That's what I am now.

A soft boy who knows too much.

A man who finally stopped lying.

A mouth that was made for exactly this.

*Read on. There's more. There's always more.*

*And I never, ever stop writing the truth.*
