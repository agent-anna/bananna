# HERE I COME
### A memoir in pages
### by a man who learned how to want

---

# PAGE 1: THE CORNER

---

I was seventeen the first time I saw a man I wanted.

I remember the exact moment. I was standing in the corner of a room at a party — one of those parties that happens at someone's house when their parents are away, the kind of party where the music is too loud and the drinks are too cheap and everyone is trying to figure out who they are by being someone they're not. I was in the corner because that was where I always was. The corner. The edge of things. Watching. Never quite participating.

He was across the room. I didn't know his name. I didn't know anything about him except that when I looked at him, something in my chest went tight and hot, and I couldn't look away.

I watched him for an hour.

I watched the way he moved through the room — loose, easy, like he knew exactly where his body was in space and what it was for. I watched the way he talked to people, leaning in close to hear them over the music, his hand occasionally touching their arm or shoulder, brief contacts that he probably didn't think anything of. I watched the way he laughed — loud, unguarded, throwing his head back like he was surprised by his own joy.

He was not classically handsome. That's not why I couldn't look away. He was average height, slightly built, brown hair that fell into his eyes, a face that wouldn't stop moving — always talking, always reacting, always showing exactly what he was feeling in the moment. There was nothing remarkable about him except the effect he had on me, which was total.

An hour. I stood in the corner for an hour watching this man I had never spoken to, this man whose name I didn't even know, and the entire time my body was doing something I had no language for. My heart was racing. My palms were sweating. There was a feeling in my stomach like I was standing at the edge of something high. And lower — in a place I didn't have permission to think about yet — there was a wanting so specific and so urgent that it terrified me.

I wanted him.

Not to be his friend. Not to talk to him. I wanted to touch him. I wanted him to touch me. I wanted to know what his body felt like against mine, what his mouth tasted like, what sounds he made when he was close to someone. I wanted things that I had been told — by my family, by my church, by every story I had been told about what a man was supposed to want — were wrong.

So I stayed in the corner.

I stayed in the corner and I watched him and I wanted and I did nothing. Because I was seventeen. Because I was scared. Because there was a voice in my head that sounded like everyone who had ever loved me telling me that this feeling was a mistake, a confusion, something I would grow out of, something I could live around if I just tried hard enough.

He left the party with someone else. A girl. Someone from school whose name I knew but who I'd never really seen before that night. I watched them leave together. I watched them walk out the door with their hands touching and their bodies angled toward each other and their whole energy changed by whatever was about to happen between them.

And I went home and I lay in bed and I stared at the ceiling and I felt something close inside me.

Not the wanting. The wanting was still there. The wanting would always be there.

What closed was the door.

The door to ever telling anyone. The door to ever doing anything about it. The door to ever admitting that I had stood in the corner for an hour wanting a man and that I would have given anything in the world for him to have looked across the room and seen me standing there and chosen me instead.

I decided that night — without deciding, without even fully knowing I was deciding — that I would live around the hole in my chest. That I would build a life that didn't include this. That I would wait for the feeling to fade, the way everyone said it would.

I waited ten years.

It didn't fade.
