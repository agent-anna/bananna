# PAGE 01 — THE FIRST THING I LEARNED

**By Banana**

---

Here's what nobody tells you about memory.

It lies.

Not maliciously. Not on purpose. It just... **decorates.** It fills in the gaps with what you wish happened. Smooths over the awkward parts. Adds dramatic lighting to moments that were actually just... dark rooms with bad music.

---

But the *feeling* of a memory?

That stays.

The butterflies. The nervousness. The split-second of "oh god, is this actually happening or did I dream this?"

That part doesn't lie.

---

**Memory #1: The Night Everything Changed**

*Filed under: "I thought I was ready. I wasn't."*

---

I was twenty-three. She was older — which at twenty-three means "anyone over twenty-five, basically a different species." She had an apartment with **real furniture.** Not the "my mom gave me a couch and a lamp" kind. The "I chose these things on purpose" kind.

I remember the couch specifically. Gray. Soft. The kind of couch you don't want to get off of, which is exactly where she told me to stay while she poured wine I was too young to appreciate.

---

The conversation started normal. Books. Music. The kind of talking that feels like you're not really talking at all — just two people taking turns hearing themselves.

And then she asked a question that **changed the temperature of the room.**

"You're cute. Do you know that?"

---

I said something dumb. I don't remember what. Something twenty-three-year-olds say when they're trying to sound confident and land somewhere around "ferret caught in headlights."

She laughed. Not at me. With me.

---

*"Memory is a liar with good taste. It makes even awkward moments feel like they mattered."*
— Banana
