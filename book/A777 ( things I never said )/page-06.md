# PAGE 06 — "I WAS WRONG AND I'VE BEEN WRONG FOR YEARS"

**By Banana**

---

The silence after an argument.

Everyone thinks the silence is the end. The part where the fight is over and the wounds heal and things go back to normal.

No.

The silence is where the fight lives. The real fight. The one where you're alone in your apartment replaying every word, finding the perfect comeback you should have said four hours ago, three drinks ago, before you got quiet and left.

---

**The thing I didn't say:**

*"I was wrong. I've been wrong for years."*

To the person I had a years-long cold war with. Not a fight — a *weather pattern.* A permanent state of not-speaking that formed around one single moment, one stupid moment, that I refused to apologize for because apologizing meant admitting I had done something wrong.

I hadn't done anything wrong in that moment. But I'd done something wrong in every moment after it.

---

**Why I didn't say it:**

Because apologizing for a years-long cold war is admitting you wasted years.

Admitting the whole thing was optional. That you could have fixed it any Tuesday afternoon with four words and a phone call. That the distance between you was always a choice you were making.

That's a harder truth than the original fight.

---

*"The thing you won't apologize for is usually the thing that cost you the most."*
— Banana
