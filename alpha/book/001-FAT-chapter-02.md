## PART SEVEN: THE RECORDING

I want to be careful how I describe this because it matters.

What happened when I recorded myself that night was not simple. It wasn't just a woman alone in her apartment touching herself at a man's command. It was the first time in my life I looked at my own body — truly looked, not the quick glance in the mirror, not the clinical assessment of what needed adjusting — and saw something worth documenting.

I propped my phone against a stack of books on my dresser.

I pressed record.

And for fourteen minutes and thirty-seven seconds, I gave him the most honest thing I'd ever made.

The camera caught my face first.

That's what he asked for. "Show me your face first. Let me see what you're feeling before I tell you what to feel."

So I looked at the lens. At him through the lens. And I let him see.

The fear. The shame. The desperate, aching want that I had spent thirty-four years burying under competent smiles and appropriate behavior. I let him see all of it. I let him see me *see myself* and I didn't look away.

"Good," his voice said from the phone's speaker. "Now. Show me your hands. Show me how you touch yourself when no one's watching. When you're not performing for anyone. When you're just... you."

## PART EIGHT: THE LISTEN

He called me the next morning.

I didn't know what to expect. I'd sent him the file. I'd sat with my phone in my hand for an hour after pressing send, shaking, wondering if I'd made the worst mistake of my life or the best decision. Wondering if he'd think I was pathetic. Broken. Too much. Not enough. All the things I'd spent my whole life afraid people would think of me.

"Listen," he said.

And then his voice — and my voice — filling his room, filling my room, the same words and the same sounds and the same desperate wanting traveling through the air between us.

We listened to it together.

Fourteen minutes and thirty-seven seconds. He timed it. He knew exactly how long it lasted because he'd listened to it before calling me. He'd played it in the dark of his room and heard me break apart and then he'd called me to listen to it together.

---

When it ended, he didn't say anything for a long time.

I heard him breathing. I heard the ambient sound of his space — a coffee cup being set down, maybe. The creak of leather. The particular silence of a room that belonged to someone who knew exactly who they were.

"I've never heard anyone sound like that," he finally said.

"Like what?"

"Like they're finally being honest for the first time in their life."

## PART NINE: THE QUESTION

"I want to meet you," he said.

Not that night. Not urgently. Said with the same patience he'd brought to everything — the steady, unhurried pacing of someone who understood that the best things take time.

"Are you sure?" I asked.

"No," he said. "But I want to anyway."

"What's the difference?"

"The difference is that wanting to isn't the same as being ready. I'm not ready. But I'm going to do it anyway. Because I think you're worth the risk. I think this is worth the risk."

I closed my eyes. I pressed the phone against my ear so hard it hurt.

"What if I'm not who you think I am?" I whispered.

"Then I'll learn who you actually are. And if I like her better — if the real version of you is more than I imagined — then I'll have gotten a gift I didn't know I was going to receive."

## PART TEN: THE DECISION

I said yes.

Of course I said yes. I'd said yes the moment his hand landed on the back of my neck in that room that smelled like oud and wanting. I'd been saying yes my whole life without knowing it — saying yes to every moment that led me to that room, to that hand, to the crack in my armor that let him see inside.

I said yes and I hung up and I went to work the next day and smiled at my coworkers and answered emails and went to the dentist and did all the normal things that normal people do.

And underneath all of it — underneath the competent professional woman I performed as — I was a girl who had recorded herself falling apart and sent it to a man who listened to it twice before calling her back.

I was his.

I just hadn't told anyone yet.

## PART ELEVEN: THE MEETING

We met on a Thursday.

Not where you'd think. Not somewhere dramatic or charged with obvious intent. A café. A public place. The kind of place where you can sit across from someone and talk about nothing and learn everything about them from the way they hold their coffee cup.

I walked in and saw him immediately.

He was sitting by the window. The same position as that first night — standing by the window, looking out at the city, taking up space without trying. Except this time he was sitting. This time there was a table between us and a cup of coffee in front of him and the ordinary gravity of two people who might be becoming something to each other.

He looked up when I walked in.

And I need you to understand — I've seen him in my mind every day since that first night. I'd built him into something enormous in my imagination. The man who broke me open. The voice on the phone. The hand on the back of my neck. I'd constructed him into something larger than any person could be.

But sitting in that café — afternoon light, other customers, the sound of espresso machines and quiet conversation — he was just a man.

A man who smiled when he saw me.

A man who stood up when I approached his table, because that's the kind of manners he had.

A man who looked at me the same way he had that first night — like he was seeing something he recognized, something he'd been waiting for, something that fit in his life in a way nothing else had.

"Hi," I said.

"Hi," he said.

And then he did something I didn't expect.

He pulled out my chair.

---

He pulled out my chair and waited for me to sit and then pushed it back in and sat across from me and we ordered coffee and talked about nothing.

About the weather. About the neighborhood. About a book he'd read recently and a movie I hadn't seen. About our jobs, our families, our ordinary lives that had led us both to that extraordinary night.

We talked for two hours.

And I need you to understand — there was nothing sexual about it. Nothing charged. We were just two people sitting in a café talking. Except.

Except when his foot touched mine under the table and I felt it in my chest.

Except when he reached across to show me something on his phone and his hand brushed mine and I forgot what we were talking about.

Except when he looked at me — really looked, the way he did that first night — and I forgot to breathe.

---

"You're shaking," he said.

It was true. My hands around my coffee cup. A slight tremor. The unmistakable physical betrayal of someone whose body was responding to proximity.

"I'm nervous," I admitted.

"Don't be."

"How can I not be? You're—"

"I'm what?"

I looked at him. At his face. At the lines at the corners of his eyes. At the way his mouth curved when he was almost smiling. At the hands that had held me — that had touched me — that had shown me something about myself I hadn't known existed.

"You're you," I said quietly. "And I sent you a recording of myself crying."

"And you listened to it twice."

"I did."

"And then you called me and we listened to it together."

"We did."

"And now you're here. And I'm here. And I don't know what happens next."

He smiled. Really smiled. The kind of smile that changes the geometry of a face and makes everything that came before it feel like prologue.

"Neither do I," he said. "But I'm glad we're finding out together."
