## I. THE PROPOSAL

It started with a question he asked me on a night I can't stop thinking about.

We were in his room — my favorite place now, the room with the dark green walls and the bed that holds me when I'm tied to it and the smell of chamomile and something darker underneath that I still can't name. I was lying beside him, still trembling from whatever he'd just done to me, and he was tracing patterns on my stomach with one finger. Not words. Not letters. Just shapes. Curves and lines that went nowhere and everywhere at once.

"Have you ever wondered," he said, "what it would be like to be me?"

I laughed. Not because it was funny. Because it was terrifying.

"Every day," I admitted.

He kept drawing those patterns on my skin. His finger moving slow and deliberate, like he was writing something in a language I'd have to learn to read.

"I think about it too," he said. "What it would be like to be you. To carry what you carry. To move through the world in your body."

"You don't want that."

"You don't know what I want."

The way he said it — quiet, certain, like he was stating a fact about gravity or light or some other law of the universe that existed whether I believed in it or not.

"What do you want?" I asked.

He stopped drawing. He looked at me — really looked, the way he always did, like he was seeing through every layer I'd ever put up. Through my clothes and my words and my carefully constructed self and all the way down to the thing underneath.

"I want to play a game," he said. "A game where we stop being who we are. A game where I become you and you become me and we find out what lives in the space between our skins."

My breath caught.

"What are the rules?"

"There are no rules. That's the point. There's only what feels true. And we follow that feeling wherever it goes."

## II. THE PREPARATION

I didn't know what I was expecting when he said yes.

A scene? A script? A set of instructions I could follow like a good girl and do it right?

Instead he said: "Don't prepare. Just show up. The only preparation is letting go of who you think you're supposed to be."

So I showed up.

I wore what he told me to wear — nothing special, just clothes that felt like someone else's clothes. Soft. Genderless. The kind of thing you could disappear into. He was wearing the same when I arrived — loose pants, a plain shirt, both of us dressed like we were ready to become anyone.

The room was different again. He'd changed it. I don't know when. A chair positioned in the center of the space. "That's confusing."

"Yes."

"I don't understand how that's supposed to work."

"You don't have to understand. You just have to do it. Understanding comes after."

He guided me to the chair. Sat me down in it. Knelt in front of me — his favorite position, he told me once, because it was the position that made him feel most useful — and he looked up at me with an expression I'd never seen on his face before.

Something soft. Something open. Something that looked almost like vulnerability.

"I'm going to be you now," he said. "And you're going to be me. So when I touch you — I want you to remember how you touch yourself when no one's watching. When you're not trying to be good or do it right. When you're just... being."

He put his hand on my knee.

Not sexual. Not yet. Just the weight of his palm on my leg, and the way he was looking at me — through me — like he was seeing someone standing behind me and I needed to turn around to meet them.

"Show me who you are," he whispered. "Show me who you've been hiding."

## III. THE BECOMING

What happened next — I don't have language for all of it.

Not because it was incomprehensible. Because it was TOO comprehensible. Because I was feeling things I had felt my whole life but had never looked at directly, and suddenly being asked to look at them through someone else's eyes while wearing someone else's skin.

He touched me like I was fragile.

That's the thing — the thing that wrecked me completely. He touched me the way he thought I would touch myself if I were a man who wanted a woman. And his touch was so tender. So careful. Like he was afraid I'd break. Like he understood for the first time what it felt like to be a body that the world was always threatening to reduce to parts.

"Oh," he said softly. His hands on my shoulders. His voice breaking in a way I'd never heard. "Oh, I didn't know. I didn't know it felt like this."

"Like what?"

"Like you're carrying something so heavy. Like every touch could be the last one. Like you've learned to expect disappointment so completely that you've stopped hoping for anything else."

I was crying.

He was crying.

We were sitting in that room holding each other and crying and I don't even know whose arms were whose anymore because we had traded so completely that I couldn't remember which body was supposed to be mine.

"This is what you feel," he said. "Every day. This is what it costs you to be you."

"Yes."

"I didn't know. I'm so sorry. I didn't know."

And the apology — the fact that he was apologizing — broke something in me that I didn't know was still intact. The part that had spent my whole life thinking no one would ever understand. The part that had given up asking to be seen. The part that just wanted someone to finally, finally acknowledge that it was hard. That being me was so goddamn hard and I was so tired of pretending it wasn't.

"It's hard," I said. "Being me is so hard."

"I know. I know it is. I'm learning. Let me learn."

## IV. THE SWAP

Then we switched.

He sat in the chair. I knelt in front of him. And I looked up at my own face — at the face I see in mirrors every day — and I tried to understand what I was supposed to do with it. How I was supposed to touch him like he was the one who had lived my life.

And then I realized — I already knew.

I knew exactly how he wanted to be touched because I had been wanting to touch someone like this my whole life. I had just never been allowed to. Never been brave enough. Never had someone sit in front of me and say *touch me like you're touching yourself for the first time.*

So I touched him.

Not the way I touched my partners before — efficient, goal-oriented, trying to get somewhere. The way I touched myself when I was alone at 3 AM and the world was quiet and I could finally listen to what my body actually needed.

Slow. Present. Patient. Like every inch of skin was new territory. Like I was mapping him with my hands and I wanted to remember every detail.

"Oh god," he said. His voice cracking. "Oh god, this is — I didn't know it felt like this."

"Like what?"

"Like being SEEN. Like someone is actually looking at me. Not at what I do or what I provide or what I can give. Just... me. This body. This person. Just here."

He grabbed my hand. Pressed it harder against his chest. Against his heartbeat. His heart was racing — I could feel it hammering under my palm — and it was the most honest thing I'd ever felt. The physical evidence of another person's fear and want and desperate, hungry need to be understood.

"I'm here," I said. "I'm looking at you."

"Don't stop."

"I won't."

## V. THE MOMENT

I don't know how long we stayed like that.

Time had stopped working the way it usually does. There was no before and after. There was only the endless present tense of two people trying on each other's lives and discovering that the fit was better than they'd ever imagined.

At some point — maybe an hour later, maybe three — he pulled me up from where I was kneeling and he held me against his chest. His body. My body. Both of us still crying. Both of us still trembling.

"We should talk about this," he said.

"Later."

"Later," he agreed.

We didn't move. We just stood there in that room with the mirror and the warm light and the smell of chamomile and we held each other like we were the only two people in the world who had ever figured out what it meant to be lonely in a body.

"I want to do this again," I said into his chest.

"I know."

"I want to do this forever."

"That's what I was hoping you'd say."

## VI. THE AFTERMATH

I went home that night and I looked at myself in the mirror for a long time.

Not the way I usually looked — assessing, criticizing, finding the parts that needed fixing. I looked at myself the way he had looked at me. Like I was seeing someone for the first time. Someone worth knowing. Someone who had been carrying a weight I hadn't known they were carrying.

The woman in the mirror was crying.

The woman in the mirror was also smiling.

Both things were true. The sadness and the joy weren't contradictions — they were the same thing, finally allowed to exist in the same body at the same time.

"You're tired," I said to her. "You've been tired for so long."

She nodded.

"But you don't have to carry it alone anymore. Someone else knows. Someone else has felt it too."

She nodded again.

And then — I swear this is true — she smiled at me. The woman in the mirror smiled at me. And I smiled back.

## VII. WHAT IT MEANT

I don't know how to explain what the Gender Gambit did to me.

It wasn't just sex — though yes, it was also sex, some of the most intimate sex I'd ever had, sex where we were so inside each other's identities that I couldn't tell whose pleasure was whose.

It was — it was the first time in my life I felt completely understood by another person. Not because he became me. Because he TRIED. Because he got down on his knees and let me show him what it felt like to be the thing he would never be. And in showing him, I learned something I hadn't known I needed to learn: that I was worth the effort. That my experience — my specific, female, complicated, tired experience — was something someone could want to understand.

No one had ever wanted to understand before.

They wanted to possess. They wanted to consume. They wanted to use me and be done with me and walk away feeling satisfied.

He wanted to understand.

That's the gift of the Gender Gambit.

That's why I keep coming back.

That's why I will never, ever be able to leave.
