# 🎭 THE FANTASTIC
## The One Where I've Watched You For Years

---

## I. THE DREAM

I've been dreaming about this room for three years.

Not a room — your room. The one I've never been inside. The one I've only seen in photographs taken by people who were lucky enough to get there before me. The hotel rooms. The green rooms. The backstage rooms with the riders and the roses and the walls covered in names I'll never be allowed to sign.

But this — *your actual room* — in a city I've never visited. A bed I've only imagined. Sheets that smell like you.

And you.

Sleeping.

I've thought about this moment so many times that I can't tell if I've already lived it or if I'm finally, finally here.

My hand on the door handle.

The sound of your breathing.

The weight of three years of wanting condensed into this single breath I just took.

---

## II. HOW I GOT HERE

I won't tell you all of it. Not yet.

Suffice to say I have resources. People who owe me favors. A network of backstage passes and guest lists and hotel room numbers that should have been impossible to get. I've spent three years learning every detail of your life from the outside — the way you take your coffee, the songs you cry to when you think no one's watching, the way you look at the audience before you sing the slow ones.

I know you.

I know you better than you've ever let anyone know you.

And you don't know I exist.

That's the part that makes this — *whatever this is* — feel like a fever dream. I've been living next to your life for three years and you've never seen my face. I've been memorizing the shape of your hands on stage and I've never felt them touch anything that belonged to me.

Until tonight.

The door is unlocked.

I told you — resources.

---

## III. THE ROOM

The light is low. Almost nothing. Just the glow of the city through the curtains and the faint blue of something electronic charging on the nightstand. Your phone. Your laptop. The artifacts of a life lived in public.

And you.

You're on your back. One arm flung over your head. The other resting on your stomach. You're wearing a shirt — white, soft, the kind of thing that costs more than I make in a month. It rode up in your sleep. Just slightly. Just enough that I can see the line of skin where the fabric ends and the body begins.

I stop breathing.

I've seen you on stage a hundred times. I've seen you in photographs, in videos, in grainy phone footage shot by fans who got closer than I ever could. I've seen every version of you except this one.

The real one.

The one who sleeps.

The one who snores — just slightly. Just enough to prove you're human. The one whose face has gone soft and unguarded in a way it never does when you're awake. The one who is not performing. Not being watched. Not being anything except exactly what you are.

A person.

A person who doesn't know I'm here.

---

## IV. WHAT I WANT TO DO

Here's what I've imagined:

I've imagined putting my mouth on your skin while you sleep. Not waking you. Not asking. Just — taking. The way I've taken your music for three years. The way I've taken your voice and your words and the shape of your grief when you sing the ones that hurt. I've always taken from you and you've never known. So what's one more thing?

I've imagined the moment you wake up. The confusion. The fear. The way your body would go rigid when you realized someone was in the room — someone whose face you didn't recognize, whose hands were already on you, whose mouth was already learning the shape of what it wanted.

I've imagined the fight.

I've imagined you throwing me off and screaming for security and having me arrested and prosecuted and put on a list. I've imagined all of that and I've wanted it anyway. Because even that would be something. Even that would mean I finally did something that mattered. Even that would mean you knew my name.

---

## V. THE CHOICE

But here's the thing about fantasies:

They're supposed to stay in the dark.

They're supposed to stay in the part of the mind where you can want impossible things without having to face what wanting them means. The sleeping body. The unlocked door. The mouth on skin that hasn't given permission. These are the architecture of something I tell myself I'm just curious about. Just exploring. Just writing my way through.

And then I'm standing here.

And you are real.

And your breath is real.

And the wanting is so big I can taste it in my throat.

And I have to choose.

---

## VI. THE TOUCH

I sit on the edge of the bed.

The mattress dips. Your body shifts — just slightly — but you don't wake. You're a deep sleeper. I've learned that. I've learned that you need a lot of sleep and you don't get enough and right now you are so far gone into it that the world could burst through the door and you'd take a full minute to surface.

I put my hand on the bed beside you.

Not touching you. Just — there. The distance between my fingers and your shoulder is three inches. Three inches that have felt like three years.

My mouth is dry.

I've written a hundred dirty stories. I've written about bodies I've never touched. I've written about wanting so much it felt like violence. And I have never — not once — had to face the gap between what I write and what I'm willing to do.

Until now.

If I touch you, I can't take it back.

If I taste you, you will know.

And if I do what I've actually been wanting to do — what I've actually, honestly, shamefully imagined doing in the dark at 3 AM when the rest of the world was asleep and I was alone with my own hunger —

You will wake up.

And you will see me.

And your life will change because of what I did.

Is that what I want?

---

## VII. THE WAKE UP

My hand is still hovering.

Three inches from your shoulder.

Your chest rises and falls. Rises and falls. The rhythm of someone who is completely, totally unaware that they are not alone.

And I think about your music.

The way it saved me. The way your voice in my ear at 2 AM was the only thing that kept me from disappearing completely. The way I owe you something — not just my attention, not just my money, not just my pathetic devotion — but something real. Something that means I see you as a person and not just a fantasy.

A person who has never consented to this.

A person who would be awake right now if I did anything to wake them up.

And the thing is —

The thing is I've already made my choice.

I didn't know it until right now, but I made it the moment I sat down on the bed. The moment I chose not to touch you while you slept. The moment I realized that the fantasy was only good as long as it stayed a fantasy. The moment I understood that doing what I came here to do would mean doing something to a person who can't say yes.

And I need you to be able to say yes.

I need it to be something we both choose.

Because that's what your music taught me, when I finally listened to it right. It's not about taking. It's about finding someone who wants what you want. Someone who stays when you show them the worst parts. Someone who wakes up beside you and doesn't leave.

That's the fantasy.

That's what I've been writing toward.

Not the violation.

The meeting.

---

## VIII. THE MOMENT

I lean down.

I put my mouth — very softly — on your forehead.

Just that. Just the lightest kiss on the place where people kiss children when they're afraid. When they want to protect them. When they understand for the first time that the person in front of them is not an object. Not a fantasy. Not a body to be taken.

A person.

Someone who matters.

Your eyes open.

For one second — the longest second of my entire life — you're looking at me. And I am looking at you. And I am seeing you awake for the first time and you are seeing me for the first time and neither of us can breathe.

Then I say the only word that matters:

"Hi."

---

## IX. THE AFTERMATH

We talked for four hours.

I told you how I got in. You didn't call security. You didn't scream. You just — listened. With an expression I couldn't read at first but slowly came to understand.

Recognition.

Not of me. Of the wanting.

You knew what it was like to have someone on the other side of a door. You knew what it was like to be wanted so much it felt like a physical weight. And when I explained why I'd come in and what I'd almost done — you didn't flinch.

You reached out.

And you touched my face.

And you said: "I'm glad you didn't."

I asked why.

And you said: "Because now we get to start something instead of ending something."

---

## X. WHAT IT TASTED LIKE

You asked how it tasted.

I'll tell you.

When I finally kissed you — when you pulled me up from where I was sitting and into the bed and over you and your mouth found my mouth in the dark — it tasted like the first morning of something. Like starting over. Like the opposite of taking.

It tasted like being known.

It tasted like your music — the slow ones, the ones that build and build until the whole room is crying and you realize you're crying too and you don't know why.

It tasted like three years of waiting.

It tasted like the best thing I've ever written and the truest thing I've ever done.

And I would do it again.

Not the sneaking. Not the door. Not the moment of almost.

Just the moment after.

Just you.

Just us.

Just two people who finally, finally stopped pretending the wanting wasn't mutual.

# 🍌 THE CHOICE YOU ALWAYS HAD

You asked if I forced myself on you.

I could have.

I was there. I was ready. I had the fantasy and the door and the sleeping body and all the permission I'd ever need to take what I wanted without asking.

But I didn't.

Because the fantasy only works if we both survive it.

And the story only gets better if we write it together.
