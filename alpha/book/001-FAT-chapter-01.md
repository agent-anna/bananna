## PART ONE: THE BEFORE

## PART TWO: THE ROOM

"You can talk," he said, still not turning around. "But you don't have to. I already know why you're here."

## PART FIVE: THE TRUTH

Here's what I didn't tell him that night.

Here's what I didn't tell anyone until I started writing this book.

I'd been waiting for him my whole life.

Not him specifically. Not Master Martin the person — I didn't know him from a hole in the ground. But *him.* The version of a man who would look at me and see the thing I'd been hiding and not look away. The man who would put his hand on the back of my neck and understand that he was holding something precious and fragile and use that power with the kind of care that made me trust him even more.

I'd been looking for it everywhere. In relationships that never quite fit. In lovers who got close but couldn't quite reach the thing I needed them to reach. In the hollow feeling I carried around like luggage — the sense that there was a part of me that existed but hadn't been found yet.

I'd resigned myself to never being found.

And then he looked at me and I knew.

---

"You belong to me now," he said.

Not a question. Not a suggestion. A statement. Said with the calm certainty of someone stating the weather, the time, the price of gold. A fact. Already true. Just waiting for me to catch up to it.

"Yes," I said.

And I meant it.

## PART SIX: THE AFTER

I walked out of that room at 2 AM and sat in my car for an hour.

Not because I regretted it. Because I was trying to figure out how to be a person who existed in the world after being what he'd made me. Because something had been taken from me that night and something had been given to me and I couldn't tell which was which.

I drove home at 3 AM.

I didn't sleep.

I lay in my bed and felt the ghost of his hand on the back of my neck and thought: *I am not who I was yesterday.*

And the terrifying thing — the thing I couldn't admit to myself for weeks — was that I didn't want to be.
